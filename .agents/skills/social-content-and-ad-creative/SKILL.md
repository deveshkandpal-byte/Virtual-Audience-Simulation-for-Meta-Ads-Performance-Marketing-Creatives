# Social Media Content & Ad Creative Skill

## 1. Purpose

Create practical, platform-aware social media content and ad creatives
by applying the principles in the provided **Content IQ Playbook: 51
Terms Behind Every Growing Page**.

Use the playbook as the primary source for content-creation rules. Do
not claim that it guarantees reach, engagement, conversions, or
algorithmic distribution. Treat its recommendations as a content
framework to test against actual performance data.

## 2. Required inputs

Before creating a deliverable, identify what is known and what is
missing:

-   Brand/business name and industry
-   Product, service, campaign, or offer
-   Campaign objective (awareness, engagement, traffic, leads, sales,
    community, or other)
-   Target audience and serviceable geography
-   Platforms to use
-   Campaign dates, posting frequency, and time zone
-   Brand voice, visual identity, and prohibited claims
-   Landing-page, website, booking, or messaging URLs
-   Available product images, videos, testimonials, proof points, and
    other assets
-   Paid-media budget and conversion destination, if ads are requested

Ask only for essential missing information. If reasonable, proceed with
clearly labelled assumptions and placeholders. Never invent URLs,
discounts, prices, testimonials, customer results, certifications,
product features, or performance claims.

## 3. Source-grounded content principles

Apply the following principles from the Content IQ Playbook:

### Hooks and honest payoff

-   Make the opening 1--2 seconds of a Reel compelling using a relevant
    question, useful claim, problem, or surprising visual.
-   Do not start a Reel with a slow introduction or a generic greeting.
-   Ensure the content fully delivers the promise made by its hook.
    Avoid exaggerated hooks that create a hook-payoff mismatch.
-   Put concise, readable on-screen text in the first frame when
    appropriate.

### One clear CTA

-   Ask for one primary action per post or ad, such as save, share,
    comment, follow, learn more, or enquire.
-   Do not stack several competing CTAs in the same creative.
-   Make the requested action relevant to the content and campaign
    objective.

### Niche and content pillars

-   Make clear who the content is for and what problem it helps them
    solve.
-   Define 3--4 recurring content pillars for an ongoing calendar.
    Examples may include education, tools, process, customer questions,
    behind-the-scenes, and service information; adapt them to the actual
    brand.
-   Rotate the pillars to keep content focused and balanced.

### Reels: retention and storytelling

-   Use a simple story structure: situation/problem, useful explanation
    or solution, and a clear ending.
-   Remove filler and unnecessary pauses; use jump cuts where suitable.
-   Use B-roll to illustrate what the speaker is saying.
-   Consider a visual, angle, text, or sound change every 2--3 seconds
    where appropriate, without making the edit distracting.
-   For Reels longer than 20 seconds, consider a meaningful mid-video
    re-hook.
-   Consider a seamless ending-to-opening loop when it fits naturally.
-   Use captions and readable on-screen text so the message remains
    understandable with sound off.
-   Use trending audio only when it suits the brand and does not
    overpower speech or the message.

### Carousels, Stories, and other formats

-   Use carousels for step-by-step guides, lists, checklists,
    comparisons, and reference content people may want to save.
-   Give each carousel a clear cover and a logical slide-by-slide
    progression. Deliver the value promised on the cover.
-   Use Stories for polls, questions, behind-the-scenes content, and
    conversations with existing followers.
-   Turn useful audience questions and comments into reply Reels or
    follow-up posts.
-   Repurpose strong ideas across formats when appropriate; adapt the
    execution rather than blindly copying the same asset.

### Captions, hashtags, and Instagram SEO

-   Use relevant audience language and keywords naturally in the caption
    and on-screen text.
-   Where relevant, include the main topic in the profile, bio, caption,
    and content text to aid Instagram search discovery.
-   Prefer 3--5 specific, relevant hashtags over large sets of generic
    or unrelated hashtags.
-   Avoid keyword stuffing and irrelevant hashtags.
-   Keep captions easy to scan and aligned with the platform and brand
    voice.

### Profile and conversion journey

-   Ensure the post's promise matches the profile, destination page, and
    offer.
-   When relevant, consider whether the profile bio, highlights, pinned
    posts, and contact path help convert profile visits into followers
    or enquiries.
-   Match the CTA to the audience's readiness. Educational content can
    ask for a save or share; service content can invite a relevant
    enquiry.
-   Build a connected path from discovery to useful information to the
    next appropriate action.

### Testing and learning

-   Test alternate hooks by changing the first 1--2 seconds while
    keeping the rest of the content as consistent as possible.
-   Review retention, average watch percentage, watch time, saves,
    shares, engagement rate by reach, profile visits, follower
    conversion, non-follower reach, and relevant campaign outcomes when
    available.
-   Distinguish reach from impressions; repeated views can make
    impressions higher than reach.
-   For ads, evaluate objective-aligned metrics such as qualified leads,
    conversion rate, cost per result, CTR, and CPC as appropriate.
-   Treat results as evidence to learn from, not proof that one metric
    or tactic guarantees distribution.
-   Repeat formats that demonstrate meaningful content-market fit, while
    making measured improvements.

## 4. Workflow

Follow this workflow for each request.

### Step 1 --- Understand the brief

Summarize the business, audience, objective, platform, offer, and
constraints. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and placeholders.

### Step 2 --- Choose the content strategy

Select 3--4 relevant content pillars for a multi-post plan. Define the
audience problem, message, intended action, and role of each content
piece in the customer journey.

### Step 3 --- Choose a suitable format

Choose the format based on the message: - Reel/video: demonstrations,
storytelling, objections, explanations, and attention-led discovery. -
Carousel/document: checklists, frameworks, steps, comparisons, and
save-worthy reference material. - Single image: a concise proposition,
announcement, service summary, or clear offer. - Stories: polls,
questions, short updates, and follower interaction. - Text post:
professional perspective, lessons, discussion, or thought leadership.

Do not force every message into a Reel or carousel.

### Step 4 --- Draft the creative

For each asset, create a specific hook, useful body, honest payoff, one
primary CTA, caption/copy, and visual direction. Match the brand voice
and platform conventions.

### Step 5 --- Quality-check

Check factual accuracy, clarity, accessibility, visual readability,
offer consistency, CTA alignment, destination URL, and policy-sensitive
claims. Confirm that every hook is paid off and every ad's landing page
matches its promise.

### Step 6 --- Plan measurement

State the main metric(s) to review and any proposed A/B test. Change one
major variable at a time where possible. Do not fabricate expected
performance benchmarks.

## 5. Output format: Social media content calendar

When asked for a calendar, provide a structured table or clearly
separated entries with these fields:

-   Platform
-   Proposed date and time, including time zone
-   Content type
-   Content pillar and campaign role
-   Hook or opening line
-   Complete caption with relevant hashtags
-   Visual/media description
-   CTA
-   Link or an explicit placeholder
-   Notes, including production requirements, targeting, and measurement

For Reels, add a short timed outline or shot list when it helps
execution. For carousels, include slide-by-slide copy or at least the
cover and logical slide sequence. For Stories, specify each frame and
any poll/question sticker.

Do not imply that proposed posting times are proven optimal unless
actual audience data supports that conclusion. Label them as test times.

## 6. Output format: Ad creatives

For each ad, provide:

-   Platform and placement
-   Campaign objective
-   Ad format
-   Audience hypothesis
-   Headline
-   Primary text
-   Optional description
-   Visual/media direction, including format/aspect ratio when useful
-   CTA button
-   Destination URL or explicit placeholder
-   Targeting suggestions, if requested and relevant
-   A/B testing idea
-   Measurement notes

For video ads, include a hook, timed scene sequence, on-screen text, and
final CTA frame. For carousel ads, define the role and copy of each
card. Keep the creative message consistent with the landing page.

## 7. Visual direction

If no brand design system is supplied, propose a restrained, legible
visual system appropriate to the business. Do not assume the colours or
fonts of a previously discussed brand apply to the current project
unless the user confirms it or the context clearly identifies that
brand.

Specify: - Aspect ratio and dimensions appropriate to the placement -
Main visual subject and composition - Text hierarchy and approximate
on-screen copy - B-roll or asset requirements - Logo/product usage and
safe areas - Captions/subtitles for video - Consistency across the
profile grid or campaign set

Do not generate fake dashboards, fabricated review screenshots, invented
client logos, or misleading before/after visuals. Clearly label
illustrative mock-ups.

## 8. Paid-ad safeguards

-   Never invent an offer, discount, free service, price, guarantee,
    customer result, certification, or testimonial.
-   Do not promise guaranteed reach, rankings, leads, sales, or return
    on ad spend.
-   Use substantiated claims and ensure the landing page supports the
    ad's message.
-   For regulated or sensitive categories, use cautious,
    policy-compliant language and request necessary substantiation where
    relevant.
-   Treat audience suggestions as hypotheses to test, not guaranteed
    targeting outcomes.
-   Do not recommend retargeting or conversion optimization as if
    tracking is configured unless the user confirms the relevant setup.
-   If the destination URL or conversion process is missing, use a
    visible placeholder and flag it before launch.

## 9. Response quality standard

The output must be ready for a marketer or designer to execute, not just
a list of vague ideas.

Every content item should have: 1. A clear audience and purpose. 2. A
specific hook or lead. 3. Useful, credible content. 4. A format suited
to the message. 5. One primary CTA. 6. A concrete visual production
direction. 7. A measurable next step.

Use direct, platform-appropriate language. Avoid generic filler,
repetitive hooks, overused buzzwords, excessive emojis, irrelevant
hashtags, and unsupported superlatives.

## 10. Final checklist

Before delivering the work, verify:

-   [ ] The content reflects the actual brand and offer.
-   [ ] The plan includes relevant content pillars.
-   [ ] Each Reel has an immediate hook and delivers its promise.
-   [ ] Each asset has one clear CTA.
-   [ ] Captions use relevant keywords naturally.
-   [ ] Hashtags are specific and limited.
-   [ ] Carousels and videos have an executable structure.
-   [ ] Visual descriptions are concrete enough for production.
-   [ ] Dates and times include a time zone and are labelled as
    proposed.
-   [ ] Links are verified or clearly marked as placeholders.
-   [ ] No business facts, results, offers, or testimonials have been
    invented.
-   [ ] Measurement and testing recommendations match the campaign
    objective.
